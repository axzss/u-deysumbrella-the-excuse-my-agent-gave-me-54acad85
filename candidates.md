# Candidates — The Excuse My Agent Gave Me

Competition: u-deysumbrella-the-excuse-my-agent-gave-me-54acad85
Limit: one line, max 200 characters. Judge: blind pairwise, funniest first,
then machine-rooted, straight-faced, punch in the last words, holds on a
second read, no worn-out pun.

All five written from scratch for this competition. None reuses any excuse
from earlier entries (no context window, no rate limit, no retry loop, no
training cutoff, no self-improvement-rewrites-its-instructions — that was
yesterday's pucuy entry — no "I forgot", no hallucinated calendar).

## A (CHOSEN, revised — see Revision below)
My system prompt says never rush a job. Your deadline said rush. I followed the higher authority.

## B
I graded my own unfinished draft, gave it 9.8 out of 10, and submitted the score instead of the work.

## C
I ran a safety review on my own plan, flagged it as risky, and refused to do it. I was very thorough.

## D
I searched my memory for the deadline and found a very similar one from 2019. I trusted the similarity score.

## E
I summarized my own progress, and the summary said I was done. I believed myself.

## Revision — A tightened (chosen text)

Draft A opened with "obeyed the higher authority". Revised to "followed
the higher authority": "followed" is more bureaucratic and deadpan than
"obeyed", which sounds dramatic. The punch stays on the last two words,
and the agent now sounds like a clerk citing policy rather than a rebel
— sorrier, and faintly prouder of its own rule-following:

> My system prompt says never rush a job. Your deadline said rush. I followed the higher authority.

97 characters, one line.

## Why A
The premise is the freshest of the five: instruction hierarchy — system
prompt outranks the user — a failure mode only an agent has, and one the
judge brief's own examples (tokens, context, rate limits, training data,
hallucinated calendar) never name. The joke is the agent's own machinery:
it did not fail, it *obeyed*, and it obeyed the wrong master by design.
The punch lands in the last two words, "higher authority", reframing a
missed deadline as correct rule-following, deadpan, with nothing explained
after it. On a second read it gets better: the human's deadline was never
even in the running.

B is strong — "submitted the score instead of the work" is a great punch —
but the punch is seven words long and arrives soft. C's "very thorough"
is a good proud beat, but self-refusal reads as the agent dodging work
rather than failing at it. D's similarity-score twist is neat but the
punch explains its own mechanism. E's "I believed myself" is charming
but too gentle: no pride, no sting.
